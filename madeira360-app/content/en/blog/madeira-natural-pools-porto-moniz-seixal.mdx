# Porto Moniz & Seixal: The Ultimate Guide to Madeira's Volcanic Natural Pools

Imagine this: powerful Atlantic waves crash against black basalt cliffs, sea spray explodes into the air — and just a few meters away, you're floating calmly in crystal-clear saltwater.

This isn't a dream. This is **northwestern Madeira**.

A road trip to the **natural pools of Porto Moniz and Seixal** belongs on every Madeira bucket list in 2026 more than ever. But which place is better? When is the best time to swim? And where can you capture the most epic drone shots?

In this guide, you'll find everything you need to plan your visit — based on real travel experience and up-to-date local tips.

---

## What Makes Madeira's Natural Pools So Special?

Madeira is a volcanic island. Thousands of years ago, glowing hot lava flowed into the ocean and instantly solidified when it hit the cold Atlantic water. Over time, this created dramatic rock formations that now form **natural swimming pools**.

During high tide, fresh seawater (and sometimes small fish 🐟) flows into the pools, constantly renewing the water — without the dangerous currents of the open ocean.

---

## 1. Porto Moniz: Madeira's Iconic Lava Pools

Located at the northwestern tip of the island, **Porto Moniz** is the most famous place to experience Madeira's volcanic swimming pools. What many visitors don't know: there are **two different pool areas** here.

### The "New" Natural Pools (West Side)

These are the pools you've seen on postcards and Instagram.

- **Facilities:** Changing rooms, showers, lockers, lifeguards, restaurant  
- **Accessibility:** Paved paths, wheelchair-friendly  
- **Entrance fee:** Approx. €3.00 (as of 2026)  
- **Best for:** Families, casual swimmers, anyone who values comfort and safety  

### The "Old" Pools – Piscinas Naturais do Cachalote (East Side / Harbor)

Rougher, more authentic, and less developed.

- **Facilities:** None (no showers or changing rooms)  
- **Entrance:** Free  
- **Best for:** Adventurers, photographers, and lovers of wild landscapes  

> **Insider tip:** For dramatic drone shots of waves crashing over lava edges, the free Cachalote pools are often more photogenic — fewer people, sharper rock formations, and a more raw Madeira vibe.

---

## 2. Seixal: The Wild Favorite (No Longer a Secret)

Just 10–15 minutes east of Porto Moniz lies **Seixal**, one of the most spectacular spots on the island.

Here, lush green mountains plunge almost vertically into the ocean, right next to a stunning black sand beach.

### Praia do Porto do Seixal & Natural Pools

Seixal feels more relaxed and less commercial than Porto Moniz.

- **Poça das Lesmas:** A natural lava arch creates a calm pool — perfect for underwater photos and drone footage  
- **The beach:** One of Madeira's most beautiful black sand beaches lies right next to the pools  
- **Costs:** Completely free access; a simple bar and public toilets nearby

## Porto Moniz vs. Seixal: Which One Should You Choose?

A common question: *"If I only have time for one place, where should I go?"*

**Water conditions:**
- Porto Moniz (Paid Pools): Very calm, controlled
- Seixal (Natural Pools & Beach): More natural, sometimes wavy

**Infrastructure:**
- Porto Moniz: Excellent (showers, toilets, sunbeds)
- Seixal: Basic (toilets, kiosk)

**Landscape:**
- Porto Moniz: Structured, man-made
- Seixal: Wild & dramatic (Jurassic Park vibes)

**Family-friendly:**
- Porto Moniz: Yes, very
- Seixal: Yes, but watch the waves

**Instagram factor:**
- Porto Moniz: High
- Seixal: Extremely high (black sand!)

## Practical Road Trip Tips for Northern Madeira

### 1. Check Weather & Tides

Northern Madeira is often rougher than sunny Funchal.

- **Webcams:** Use *Madeira Weather* or *MEO Beachcam* before you go  
- **Tides:**  
  - Low tide → calmer, shallower pools  
  - High tide + strong swell → Porto Moniz pools may close (red flag)

### 2. Getting There & Parking

- **Best route:** From Funchal, take the VE4 tunnel via Encumeada — fastest option (≈50 minutes)  
- **Scenic return:** The old coastal road ER101 offers jaw-dropping views (not for nervous drivers!)  
- **Parking:**  
  - Porto Moniz: large paid parking areas (cheap and convenient)  
  - Seixal: limited roadside parking — arrive early (before 10 AM) or late (after 4 PM)

### 3. Drone Etiquette in 2026

Madeira is a drone paradise — but rules are stricter now.

- Never fly directly over people in the pools  
- Porto Moniz is close to a heliport — always check **no-fly zones** in your drone app  
- Best drone shots: **Seixal at sunrise**, when light spills over the mountains and the black lava rock starts to glow

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Is the water cold?**  
Water temperatures usually range from **19°C (winter)** to **23°C (late summer)**. Refreshing, but warmer than the open Atlantic.

**Can you swim in Porto Moniz during winter?**  
Yes. Madeira is a year-round destination, though winter storms may cause temporary closures.

**Do I need water shoes?**  
- Porto Moniz paid pools: Not necessary  
- Seixal & free pools: **Highly recommended** — lava rocks can be sharp and slippery
